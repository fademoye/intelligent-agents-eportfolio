# Reflective Case Studies

## Case Study 1: Bias in Autonomous Decision Making

### What?

I examined how intelligent agents can reproduce unfair outcomes when they are trained on incomplete or biased data. I initially viewed bias mainly as a problem with datasets, but I learned that it can also arise from system objectives, design choices and evaluation methods.

### So What?

This changed how I think about responsible development. A technically accurate system may still disadvantage particular groups. Bias testing must therefore be part of requirements, development and evaluation rather than treated as a final check.

### Now What?

In future projects, I will review training data, test outputs across different user groups and document known limitations before deployment.

## Case Study 2: Explainable Agent Decisions

### What?

I considered the difficulty users may face when an intelligent agent provides a recommendation without explaining how it reached the result.

### So What?

This showed me that accuracy alone is insufficient. Users need to understand the basis of important outputs, particularly where decisions affect education, finance, employment or access to services.

### Now What?

I will design systems that provide clear reasons, source information, confidence indicators and opportunities for human review.

## Case Study 3: Responsible Deployment

### What?

I reflected on the risks of releasing intelligent systems before they have been adequately tested and monitored.

### So What?

Deployment can create privacy, security and reliability risks even when the original purpose is beneficial. Responsibility continues after release through monitoring, maintenance and user support.

### Now What?

I will use staged testing, human oversight, feedback channels and clear escalation procedures before wider deployment.
